Research program · Downtown Reno

Recursive self-use demonstration.

We test-drive the system. We do not try to understand it by thinking about it, and we do not call it validated because a paper says it should work.

A recursive self-use demonstration means three things at once. Self-use: the full-time researcher works with our own frontier AI, on the work itself. Demonstration: what that work makes is put in front of a person. Recursive: what the drive shows is taken back to the bench, and the next version is built from the drive, not from the argument.

The lab and the campus are at the El Cortez, and the men’s restroom at the Men’s Club is operated as a holographic theater and behavioral lab under the direction of Player 1, operating as Valet Pru. The El Cortez is where the car is built and driven by the person who built it. The restroom is where someone who did not build it gets in.

Why a test drive, and not a theory

Intellectual validation asks whether the account is consistent. A person can finish that work and still be wrong about what happens in a room. A test drive has a different question: what does this do when it is actually used? The researcher’s drive is at the El Cortez, with the frontier AI beside the work of architecture, software, and experiment. The public drive is at the Men’s Club. The theater plays the music, the Sonic Singularity tracks, the pictures, and the story. The behavioral lab, in that same restroom, records the useful part: where attention goes, what the person expected, and what changes when they learn the work was made with AI.

That record is useful because it changes the next build. A score on a frozen model cannot tell you which part of the encounter mattered. A stranger’s reaction can, if it is carried back the same week and the system is changed because of it. Other programs split building, showing, and studying across teams, then try to reconnect them in a paper. We keep them in one loop so the use is the validation.

The same habit is what we ask of anyone else. Tiny Footprint is not offered as an idea to agree with. Clone it and run it. The Reno loop is how we decide what is worth that run. The open test is the drive, on your machine.

Use it at the El Cortez → play it in the Men’s Club restroom → watch the person → change the system from what the drive showed → use that next version.

Community is free. No account. Clone Workstation 0.1, then compare conventional serving with Tiny Footprint on your hardware. FractiSI does not operate the box.

How a claim gets kept

Run the pipeline, or withhold the claim.

FractiAI publishes open empirical labs. FractiSI operates open source, on a freemium model, and ships a product only where those labs already have a receipt.

Tiny Footprint is the product surface of Tiny Gateway. The gateway’s public name for the method is rhyming templates: repeated structure is kept once, and a session reconstructs the part it needs. That method is documented, versioned, and runnable. It is not a claim that a catalog constant replaces laboratory physics.

What the local lab actually measured

On a laptop GeForce, through DirectML, an allocation proxy dropped about 81.4 percent. A tiny-gpt2 microbench showed 1.80× prefill, 1.37× generate, and 1.17× on a train step. An in-process suite reported an 84.5 percent aggregate that met an 80 percent research target. Those sentences stay tied to that hardware and that bench.

High-bandwidth memory, hosted frontier models, and fleet economics are not in that receipt. They are the point of the validation program.

The full supporting set is the library: the rhyme papers, the octave frame, the Wave–Node and Tiny Reality labs, and both Tiny Gateway papers.
